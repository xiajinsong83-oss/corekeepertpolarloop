Core Keeper Biomes — Inner, Outer and Sub-Biomes Explained

Last updated: 2026-10-19

Source: Biomes — Core Keeper Wiki (atma.gg) and World — Core Keeper Wiki, cross-checked against this site’s biome data. Distances are approximate tile counts from The Core.

Biomes are the geometrically defined colour-coded zones of a Core Keeper world, each with its own terrain, ores, enemy roster, fish catches and native bosses. The layout is radial and deterministic: the ring distances are the same on every world, but the angular position of each biome is randomised by the seed. This page goes one layer deeper than the World hub — it walks through every biome, lists which ore you actually mine where, and catalogues the sub-biome dungeons and rare additions that a new player never sees coming.

How the rings stack up

The biome bands sit at fixed distances from The Core. The inner three are finite circles; the three outer biomes are effectively unlimited once you breach The Great Wall.

The inner biomes

The Undergrounds (Dirt)

Your spawn biome, wrapping The Core. Its walls are soft enough to dig bare-handed (a pickaxe just does it faster). Slimes wander here and are neutral unless hit — but the Red Slimes are aggressive and should be approached carefully. Copper Ore is the signature resource.

The Clay Caves

The first band beyond the dirt. Slimes share the space with small larvae that are harmless alone but dangerous in packs, plus your first Cavelings. You need its resources to craft the tools that let you break into the Forgotten Ruins. Signature ores: Tin Ore and Gold Ore. The Larva Hive dungeons live inside this biome.

The Forgotten Ruins (Stone)

The other half of the inner band. Because the world is rotated randomly, it is common to dig out of the dirt straight into the stone biome before ever finding clay — if that happens, just return to The Core and mine in the opposite direction. Cavelings defend their territory here. Signature ores: Iron Ore and Gold Ore.

Note: this ore assignment comes from the atma.gg biome list. An earlier local data pass attributed Iron Ore to the Clay Caves and Scarlet Ore to the Forgotten Ruins; treat the Clay-vs-Ruin ore split as 数据待核 until confirmed in-game on the current version.

The three outer biomes

Once The Great Wall comes down, the three outer biomes open up counter-clockwise around the ring. Their starting angles are randomised per seed.

Azeos’ Wilderness

A lush, bright forest — one of the brightest zones in the underground. Caveling gardeners have built plantations and animal farms here, so watch for Caveling Hunters that can disguise themselves to ambush you. Signature ore: Scarlet Ore. The Mold sub-biome dungeon infests its deeper patches.

The Sunken Sea

An infinite expanse of glowing crystal-blue water dotted with Beach Sand islands and Limestone Walls. Enemies here apply a slippery movement debuff, and the biome is mostly water — boats (Scarlet Workbench) are what make it navigable. Signature ore: Octarine Ore. The Forlorn Metropolis sub-biome is its ruined-tech dungeon.

The Desert of Beginnings

Open Desert Sand Ground divided by large Sandstone Walls that conceal pockets of colourful Galaxite Ore. It is a battleground between explosive Bomb Scarabs and Caveling Assassins, and it is dotted with oases, temple ruins and intact temple dungeons. The Titan Temple and its mazes, plus the Molten Quarry sub-biome, all sit here.

Sub-biome dungeons

“Sub-biomes” are not separate rings — they are large dungeon structures embedded inside their parent biome, usually occupying a 2×2 block of chunks.

Rare and late additions

A few biomes are easy to miss because they are either update additions or only appear in small pockets:

  • Meadow Biome — added in the Paws & Claws update. A yellow, firefly-lit zone of meadow blocks and orange grasses, home to cattle like the Bambuck and Moolin. It spawns in both the Undergrounds and Azeos’ Wilderness.
  • Alien Tech Lab — small dungeons that generate rarely in the Shimmering Frontier (and in the “pockets” of Frontier that poke into the outer biomes). Some hold a Challenge Arena: a circular room with a terminal, Materializers and waves of enemies — survive the timer and a chest spawns on the terminal.
  • The Shimmering Frontier — a bright blue crystal ring around the outer biomes, source of Gleam Wood, Solarite Ore and unique gems (Jungle Emerald, Ocean Sapphire, Desert Ruby).
  • The Passage — the outermost biome, whose fossil blocks demand late-game pickaxes; source of Pandorium Ore.
  • Breaker’s Reach — the mechanical/void biome from the Void & Voltage update, far north past the Passage; source of Relucite Ore.

FAQ

Why did I dig out of the dirt straight into stone? The Clay Caves and Forgotten Ruins share the inner band but their angular positions are randomised per seed. If you never find clay, walk back to The Core and mine the other way — the Clay Caves is on the opposite arc.

Is Mold a separate biome I have to reach? No. Mold is a dungeon sub-biome embedded inside Azeos’ Wilderness. Once you are in the Wilderness, watch for the fungus-covered 2×2 chunk structures rather than looking for a new ring.

Which ore do I need to progress the pickaxe ladder? Work up the ore line as you push outward: Copper (Undergrounds) → Tin/Gold (Clay Caves) → Iron/Gold (Ruins) → Scarlet (Wilderness) → Octarine (Sunken Sea) → Galaxite (Desert) → Solarite (Shimmering Frontier) → Pandorium (Passage) → Relucite (Breaker’s Reach).

Where do I find the Meadow? It is a rare pocket biome, not a ring. It can appear in the Undergrounds near your base or inside Azeos’ Wilderness — sweep the perimeter when you enter a new patch rather than expecting a boundary line.

Core Keeper Scenes

Last updated: 2026-10-18

Scenes are the hand-designed set pieces scattered across an otherwise procedural world. The tunnels, ores and dirt around you are generated algorithmically, but a shrine, a buried chest room, a merchant’s cell or a maze is a pre-built structure stamped into the map whole. Learning to recognize them by shape turns routine digging into targeted treasure hunting.

What makes a scene different from terrain

Most of the world is rolled on the fly, but scenes follow their own rules:

  • Each scene is a fixed blueprint, placed once per world and unique unless noted otherwise.
  • They drop into an empty chunk only when a player wanders within about 30 tiles of it, or sometimes hug an existing landmark such as a boss arena, a main dungeon or Ghorm’s ring.
  • The same blueprint can mirror itself vertically or horizontally, so two copies of the same scene may look flipped on your map.
  • The larger dungeon-shaped scenes (sub-biomes included) occupy a 2×2 or 3×3 block of chunks, while small shrines fit in a single pocket.

Because they are deferred until you get close, a distant dark corner of your explored area can still be hiding an un-generated scene — which matters for how you sweep new biomes.

Early scenes in the dirt and clay

Your home biome already rewards close looking:

  • The Core is the one fixed scene in every world. The boss statues around it act as workbenches once you power The Core by inserting, left to right, Ghorm’s Horn, the Glurch Eye and the Stolen Crystal Heart.
  • Giant Mushroom areas (up to five per world, in the Undergrounds or Clay Caves) grow a Giant Mushroom that permanently raises your maximum health by 25 — these are some of the most valuable early finds, so harvest every one you spot.
  • Mysterious Doll Set shrines hold a chest with the Mysterious Doll Set, tucked away in dirt or clay pockets.
  • Sand Ruins bury a chest in the dirt holding an Ear Plate, a Geode, a Triangle Trinket and a stack of gold bars.

Forgotten Ruins and the Caveling cities

The stone biome is where scene networks get bigger. Caveling cities are loose, wall-less blobs of overlapping rooms rather than tidy dungeons, and ordinary ruined houses dot the same zone. This is also where dynamic set pieces such as the medium maze (with its Clear Gemstone) can roll — remember these are not guaranteed to appear here on every seed.

Outer-biome scenes and sub-biomes

Once you cross the Great Wall, scenes scale up in ambition. Each outer biome carries its own sub-biome dungeon plus a roster of one-off ruins:

BiomeSub-biome dungeonScene highlights
Azeos’ WildernessMoldMold dungeons, wilderness shrines
Sunken SeaForlorn MetropolisIslands, merchant cells, underwater ruins
Desert of BeginningsMolten QuarryTemples, mini temple-and-maze clusters, hidden Caveling villages

The Desert deserves special attention: its small, unlit Chipped Blade temples and the eye-shaped Thread of Fate scene are easy to skate right past, yet they gate the legendary Rune Song sword and the Oracle Deck.

Dungeons versus small scenes

It helps to keep two buckets in mind:

  • Small scenes are single rooms or shrines — a chest, a statue, a merchant. They fit in one chunk and are easy to clear.
  • Dungeons are networks of linked rooms, often several chunks wide, that wrap a boss arena or point of interest. The Larva Hive, Mold, Forlorn Metropolis and Molten Quarry are all dungeons dressed up as “sub-biomes,” and the king/queen/prince dungeons plus sites like The Vault, the Ancient Forge and the Crystal Meteor follow the same larger template.

How to hunt scenes efficiently

  • Sweep the perimeter first. When entering a new outer biome, expand an ever-widening spiral or a back-and-forth band so you leave no unexplored dark strip. Beelining straight to a boss can strand three tiny temples off to the side.
  • Stop and backtrack near landmarks. Scenes love to nestle against boss arenas, dungeons and Ghorm’s track; if you skipped a corner on the way to a fight, circle it on the return trip.
  • Tiny is easy to miss. The Chipped Blade temples and the Thread of Fate scene are deliberately small and dim — light up suspicious clearings rather than running past them.
  • Prioritize permanent upgrades. The Giant Mushroom’s +25 max health is free power that pays off all playthrough long, so grab those areas early even if you ignore everything else around them.
